Why JDF exists

The threat is documented, severe, and under-reported

A volunteer at the door and a guard hired for the holidays protect buildings, not people. They do not cover the four blocks between a synagogue and a family home. JDF was built to cover exactly that.

6,274
United States, 2025

Antisemitic incidents recorded by the ADL, the third-highest total ever. Deadly-weapon assaults rose from 23 to 32.

817
California, 2025

ADL-recorded incidents, more than any state except New York.

202
Los Angeles County, 2024

Anti-Jewish hate crimes reported to law enforcement, the highest of any religious group in the county.

80%of religious hate crimes reported in Los Angeles County in 2024 targeted Jewish people

Sources: ADL Audit of Antisemitic Incidents (2025); Los Angeles County hate crime data (2024). More than half of hate-crime victimizations are never reported to police (U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics), so official counts understate what the community faces.

How we protect

Presence before the incident

Six layers that work as one. Select any of them to see what it does on the street.

Armed patrols

Armed, marked and unmarked units are the backbone. A unit outside a synagogue as Friday services begin tells anyone watching that the location is protected, before anything happens. Motorcycle units reach crowded streets four wheels cannot.

Marked and unmarked responders

Visible uniformed responders deter. Unmarked responders observe and report. Together they cover the street between the buildings, day and night, with medical training to treat people until help arrives.

24/7 dispatch

The Public Safety Hotline is answered by dispatchers who know the neighborhood. Every criminal matter is escalated immediately to 911 and LAPD. JDF complements law enforcement and never replaces it.

What we stand for

A public safety nonprofit, not a private security company

Mission

To protect lives, deter crime, and strengthen the resilience of the communities we serve through visible, professional, and coordinated public safety operations that complement the work of law enforcement rather than attempting to replace it.

Vision

To be recognized as one of the leading nonprofit community protection organizations in the United States: continuously staffed, professionally accountable, and trusted equally by the residents we protect and by the agencies we work alongside every day.

Key values
  • Presence over reactionDeterrence costs less than response
  • Coordination, never substitutionLAPD leads every criminal matter
  • Accountability to the donorEach dollar traces to an hour of coverage
  • Professional standardsWithout exception
